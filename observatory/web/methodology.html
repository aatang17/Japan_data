Methodology and How to Use

What this product shows, where every number comes from, and how each figure is calculated.

What this is

This site tracks the Japanese Consumer Price Index using two official national tables published by the Statistics Bureau of Japan (総務省統計局): the middle-class indices (headline and exclusion-based aggregates, the ten major expenditure groups, and the ~80 category series beneath them) and the detailed item indices (~740 series down to individual goods and services — rice, electricity, mobile phone charges). Both are monthly from January 1970 to the latest published month, on the 2020 = 100 base.

Every number on the site is traceable to the official source file it came from. Nothing is smoothed, seasonally adjusted, or modelled.

How to use it

Read the current state of inflation

  1. Open the Overview. The first tile is headline CPI year-over-year for the latest published month; the next tiles show the two standard core measures, the month-over-month change, and the 3-month annualized rate.
  2. The main chart compares headline, core, and core-core. Switch the measure (YoY / MoM / 3-month annualized / index level) and the date range with the controls above the chart. Hover for exact values across all series.
  3. The What Is Driving Inflation chart splits headline YoY into the percentage-point contribution of each major group — how much of today's inflation each group is responsible for, which is different from each group's own inflation rate. The group bar chart below it can show either view.
  4. The How Broad Is Inflation chart shows what share of the ~580 individually priced items are rising at least 2% and what share are falling — whether inflation is everywhere or concentrated in a few items.

Find a specific item

  1. Open the Item Explorer and search in English (electricity), Japanese (電気代), or by item code (0001). The Categories view shows the ~80 published category aggregates; switch to Detailed items for all ~740 series down to individual goods (rice, 米類, mobile phone charges).
  2. Sort any numeric column by clicking its header. Missing values always sort last.
  3. Click a row for the item's full history since 1970, with measure controls, a chart, and its calculation and source.

Cite or export a number

  • The page URL always encodes the current view (measure, range, search, selected item) — copy it and a colleague sees exactly the same numbers.
  • Every chart has Download PNG (with the source line embedded) and Download CSV. CSV exports begin with a metadata block: provenance, formula, source ID, vintage, retrieval time, and permalink.
  • Cite the underlying data as: Statistics Bureau of Japan, Consumer Price Index, 2020-base national indices, via e-Stat (middle-class table statInfId 000032103842; detailed-item table statInfId 000032103844).

Trust labels

Every metric, chart, and export says where its number came from:

  • Official Statistic — the value exactly as published by the agency. On this site: the monthly index levels.
  • Calculated rates carry no badge — instead they carry their formula, shown next to the number under "Show calculation" and in every CSV export header. On this site: YoY, MoM, and 3-month annualized rates, each a deterministic calculation from the official index levels above.
  • Model Estimate — not used anywhere on this site today. Reserved for nowcasts or estimates, which will ship only with a published method and error history.

Calculations

  • Year over year: (index[t] / index[t−12 months] − 1) × 100
  • Month over month: (index[t] / index[t−1 month] − 1) × 100
  • 3-month annualized: ((index[t] / index[t−3 months]) ^ 4 − 1) × 100
  • Contribution of group g to headline YoY, in percentage points: weight[g] × (index[g,t] − index[g,t−12]) / (10000 × headline_index[t−12]) × 100. The ten group contributions sum to headline YoY up to a small residual caused by the rounding of the published indices and weights; the chart bundles that residual with the smallest groups so the bars always sum exactly to the headline line.
  • The Item Explorer's Contrib. pp column uses that same formula for every row, so a figure there and the same figure on the Overview can never disagree. Note that the rows of that table overlap — an aggregate such as "All items, less fresh food" contains its own components — so the column does not sum to headline YoY. Only the ten-group decomposition on the Overview does.
  • The Explorer's YoY, 〈year〉 column is simply year over year twelve months earlier: the base a year ago, shown so a rate that moved only because the comparison month changed is visible as such.
  • Breadth: over the individually priced items of the detailed-item table (aggregates excluded), the share of items with YoY at or above 2% and the share with falling prices, as % of items that have an index value in both t and t−12.

Rates are computed from the published index values, which the agency releases rounded to one decimal. The officially published change rates are computed the same way, so the figures here normally match them exactly; in edge cases the rounding of the inputs can shift a rate by ±0.1 percentage points. Where that distinction matters, use the official release — the source link is on every surface. Displayed rates are rounded to one decimal; chart tooltips and CSV exports carry more precision.

Percentage-point differences (for example a tile's "vs prior month" delta) are written pp, never % — a change of a percentage is not a percentage of a level.

Footnote flags

Two flags mark readings whose headline rate is easy to misread. Both are calculated here from published index values and are not published by the agency. Thresholds are fixed constants, so a marker means the same thing on every row.

  • † One-month step change. The 12-month move is decomposed into its twelve monthly log changes, which sum exactly to the 12-month log change. The flag is raised when the largest single month is at least 70% of the summed absolute change (max |Δln| / Σ |Δln| ≥ 0.70) and that month moved the index by at least 10%. The share test is what separates a level shift from a move that accumulated over the year: a spike that later reverts — fresh food, most commonly — puts its reversion into the denominator and falls below the threshold. Typical of an administered price, subsidy or tax that changes on a set date.
  • ‡ Index near zero. The latest index level is below 5.0 (2020 = 100). A percent change on a base that small is arithmetically unstable — a one-tenth move in the published index can read as a double-digit rate — so the rate is shown for completeness only.

A flag describes the shape of the series and nothing else. It does not assert a cause, and its absence does not mean a reading is unremarkable: a large move spread evenly across the year is not flagged, because no single month dominates it. The month and size behind each marker are in its tooltip, in the item's detail panel, and in the flags column of the filtered CSV export.

Source and provenance

  • Source tables: 消費者物価指数 全国 1 中分類指数（1970年1月〜最新月） — middle-class indices (e-Stat page) and 全国 品目別価格指数（1970年1月〜最新月） — detailed item indices (e-Stat page), both national, monthly. Each is ingested, validated, and versioned independently.
  • Pipeline: the raw CSV is downloaded from e-Stat, archived unmodified with its SHA-256 checksum, parsed, and passed through validation gates (structure, required aggregates, plausible latest period and headline level) before anything is published. A file that fails validation publishes nothing — the previous release stays live.
  • Vintages: this version of the site keeps one live vintage — the latest accepted release — plus the archived raw file for every ingestion run. Full side-by-side vintage history is on the roadmap.
  • Missing values: months where an item was not collected are shown as — and rendered as gaps in charts. A missing value is never displayed or exported as zero. Items with no value for the latest month are marked with the month their series ends.

Update cadence and staleness

The Statistics Bureau publishes national CPI roughly three weeks after the end of the reference month. Each page shows the month its data runs through and when it was ingested. If the data on screen is more than three months behind, a red banner says so — a stale surface never presents itself as current.

Known limitations of this version

  • The Tokyo flash index, regional indices, seasonally adjusted series, and goods/services splits are not yet ingested. The Overview page reads the middle-class table only.
  • Rates of change are calculated here rather than ingested from the official change-rate tables (see Calculations above for the rounding consequence).
  • One live vintage; no side-by-side base-revision comparison yet. The 2025-base transition will be handled with an explicit item mapping table before any cross-base series is shown.
  • Group contributions are computed with fixed 2020-base weights (the Laspeyres approximation the official decomposition also uses); they are not chain-linked across base revisions.

Connecting an AI assistant (MCP)

The site exposes its data to AI assistants over the Model Context Protocol at /mcp — setup steps are on the Connect your AI page, and the manual documents every lookup. The assistant reaches the data only through read-only lookups that wrap the same functions serving the public API, so it can only see numbers this site already publishes. Every lookup result carries the same trust labels described above — official index levels tagged as official, calculated rates carrying their formula — plus a permanent citation URL for the equivalent view on this site. The assistant's prose is the product of whichever AI client you connect, not of this site; check its statements against the cited pages.
