Buybacks

What each company said it would buy back, what it actually bought, and whether the shares were cancelled. Taken from the monthly buyback status report every company files while a programme runs — the authorising resolution, the acquisition period, the shares bought that month, and the shares retired outright. Exactly as filed.

Buying and Cancellation by Month

Two different acts on one time axis. Shares bought may sit in treasury indefinitely; shares retired are cancelled outright and permanently reduce the share count. The two are shown side by side and are never summed.

Announced Versus Executed

One row per authorisation, from its latest monthly report. Yen authorised is a ceiling a board voted for; yen acquired is what was bought. They are different measures and are never summed or netted.

Authorisations

Each resolution this company reported, with the acquisition period it set itself. Completion is calculated from the filed figures; the filer's own published progress percentage is in the month-by-month table below.

Month by Month

One row per monthly report. Progress is the filer's own published percentage, official as filed; where it is missing the filing published none and nothing reconciled the figures.

Treasury and Retirements

Shares held in treasury at each month end against shares outstanding, and any shares cancelled that month. Official statistics as filed.

Source: 自己株券買付状況報告書 (monthly buyback status reports) via EDINET, Financial Services Agency. Every figure links to its filing; every raw file is archived with its SHA-256. EDINET removes these filings after about a year, so this record begins on 12 August 2025 and no earlier filing is retrievable. A closed acquisition period with the authorisation unspent is not the same as a formally abandoned programme — abandonment is announced on the stock exchange's disclosure wire, which this dataset does not carry.
