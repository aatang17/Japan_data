Add this URL to your assistant as a custom connector. It gives the assistant read-only lookups over the same numbers this site publishes — 25 datasets of Japanese official statistics and company filings, from consumer prices back to 1970 to last month's board and pay disclosures.

What it covers

Twenty-five datasets in twelve sections. The assistant sees all of them through the same handful of lookups, so you never have to name a dataset yourself.

  • Prices — national CPI by category and at full item depth, monthly from January 1970, including the 582 individually priced items.
  • Monetary — the Bank of Japan's balance sheet: stocks and monthly flows of JGBs, bills, corporate bonds, ETFs, J-REITs, loans and the monetary base, from 1996.
  • Rates — the JGB yield curve, 15 maturities daily since 1974.
  • Tourism — visitor arrivals by market from 2003, and the Accommodation Survey's guest nights and occupancy by prefecture from 2011.
  • Demography — population, households and register flows by prefecture and by municipality, plus the long run back to 1975.
  • Trade — semiconductors, semiconductor equipment and silicon wafers by partner country, monthly from 2001.
  • Agriculture — farm output and input price indices, rice contract prices by variety, private rice stocks, rice production cost, and the agricultural co-operatives' segment income statement.
  • Ownership — cross-shareholdings in both directions, the shareholder register, and the 5% large-shareholding tape.
  • Governance — every director as filed with pay, and AGM voting results down to each director's own approval percentage.
  • Capital returns — buyback programmes from authorisation to execution to cancellation.
  • Assets — disclosed facilities and land, with book value, area and the rental property note.
  • Financials — statements as filed, calculated ratios with their formulas, and segment notes including named customers.

Bank-level financial statements and the FSA bad-loan series are built but not yet published on this server. The datasets directory is the authoritative list; List datasets returns the same thing to your assistant.

What to ask

  • What is Japan's headline and core inflation right now, and which groups are driving it?
  • How broad is inflation across individual items — what share is rising more than 2%?
  • How much JGB did the Bank of Japan buy last month, net of redemptions?
  • Where is the 10-year JGB yield against a year ago?
  • Which visitor markets grew fastest last month, and how full were Kyoto's hotels?
  • Who holds Mitsubishi UFJ as a policy shareholding, and what reason do the filers state?
  • Which listed companies have the oldest boards? Which directors got the lowest approval at the AGM?
  • Show me Toyota's financial statements as filed, then its ROE against its Core30 peers.
  • Which companies have the largest unspent buyback authorisations?
  • What did last month's CPI look like as of the day before the latest release?

What your assistant can see

Read-only lookups, each a thin wrapper over this site's public API — the assistant can only see numbers the site already publishes, from the same accepted release. Eight generic tools cover every dataset on the server:

  • List datasets — every dataset: what it is, its shape, and which tools apply.
  • Describe a dataset — its source, every measure with its trust label and formula, and what this server holds.
  • Search — a company by name or securities code, or a series by name or code, across all datasets.
  • Get a company — one dataset's full view, or a profile across every dataset that knows the company.
  • Get series history — published values or calculated rates, as of any date.
  • Ranked screen — the dataset's own screens: oldest boards, largest unspent buybacks, cheapest land, lowest director approval, highest ROE.
  • List peer groups — the TOPIX scale bands, JPX market segments and the industry classifications a company can be compared within.
  • Compare within a peer group — one metric across one peer group, ranked, with the group's own median and quartiles.

The dataset cards are also readable as MCP resources. The older per-dataset tools — inflation overview, contributions, breadth, cross-shareholding coverage, the boards-and-pay and financials lookups — remain available for compatibility.

The manual documents each one in detail.

Trust and limits

  • Published values are official statistics or filed figures exactly as the source released them; year-over-year and other rates, ratios and aggregates are calculated by this platform, and every lookup result carries that label plus the formula used.
  • Most lookups accept an as of date and answer as the data stood then, from the stored vintage history — so a revised number can be compared with the one that was on the screen at the time.
  • Every result includes a permanent citation URL — the page on this site showing the same view — so any figure the assistant quotes can be checked at the source.
  • Missing values are returned as missing, never as zero. Filing-derived datasets return their own coverage with every aggregate: they accumulate filing by filing and are not the whole market.
  • The assistant's prose is the product of the AI client you connect, not of this site. Check its statements against the cited pages before quoting them; the Methodology page explains every label and formula.
  • The endpoint is rate-limited to about 120 lookups a minute per connection. For bulk or programmatic access, use the public API directly.
