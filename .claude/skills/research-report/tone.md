# Register — how a research note must read

The audience is professional investors: sell-side and buy-side analysts, and the
*Asia Economics Observations* readership, which is the same people. A note is
read the way a broker's morning note is read, not the way a column is read.

This file exists because the first pass of the regional-banks note came back as
"a magazine column, not research" (2026-09-23). Nothing about its numbers was
wrong; the register was.

> **This governs the deliverable, not the chat.** Replies to the user stay short
> and plain, as CLAUDE.md requires. A research note is written for a different
> audience and does not inherit that style. Do not let the reply rules leak into
> the draft — that is exactly how the first pass went wrong.

## The rules

1. **Lead with the finding, then the evidence.** No set-up-and-reveal, no
   withholding the conclusion for effect.
2. **Third person throughout.** Never address the reader as "you". Not "the one
   worth your time"; the note says which finding is material and why.
3. **No journalistic phrasing.** Strike "the story writes itself", "cleaning up
   the bond book", "the biggest hole", "worth your time", "here is the thing".
   Say what happened in the terms the accounts use.
4. **No hooks, no rhetorical questions, no one-line paragraphs for emphasis.**
   "Both numbers are real." is a blog beat, not a sentence in a note.
5. **Standard financial vocabulary, unglossed.** unrealised losses, net interest
   income, core net business profit, ΔEVE, IRRBB, Tier 1. The reader knows these.
   Explain only what is genuinely unusual, and then once, in a clause.
6. **Measured verbs.** "suggests", "indicates", "is largely attributable to",
   "is consistent with". Not "proves", "shows conclusively", and not the breezy
   "turns out".
7. **Complete sentences.** No fragments. Sentences may be long where the
   argument is; vary the length rather than clipping everything short.
8. **Every number exactly as given**, with its unit and basis. This never
   changes — the register is the only thing this file governs.

## Worked sample — the register to match

The user supplied this rewrite of the regional-banks opening. Match it.

> **Japanese regional banks: accounting losses and regulatory rate exposure point
> to different banks**
>
> Japan's regional banks reported record net income of ¥1,804bn for the fiscal
> year ended March 2026, an increase of 88% over two years. The improvement is
> largely attributable to higher domestic interest rates, which have widened
> lending margins.
>
> This headline figure, however, understates the cost of the adjustment. In the
> same fiscal year, regional banks realised losses of ¥1,156bn on bond sales.
> This is equivalent to 106% of the cumulative increase in net interest income
> over the preceding two years. In effect, one year of bond portfolio
> restructuring more than offset two years of margin expansion.
>
> Despite these losses, regional banks increased their holdings of Japanese
> government bonds by 41% over two years, while the megabanks reduced theirs.
> Bank-level disclosures, which the published aggregates do not capture, show
> wide dispersion. Unrealised losses on bond portfolios range from less than 1%
> to 58% of net assets.
>
> Most notably, the accounting measure of bond losses bears little relation to
> the regulatory measure of interest rate risk. Under Basel requirements, each
> bank discloses the estimated change in the economic value of its banking book
> under prescribed rate shock scenarios (ΔEVE). The rank correlation between
> unrealised bond losses and ΔEVE is 0.31. Of the ten banks with the largest
> unrealised losses, only one ranks among the ten with the largest regulatory
> rate exposure.
>
> Both measures are valid, but they answer different questions. Unrealised losses
> reflect damage already embedded in the balance sheet. ΔEVE estimates
> sensitivity to further rate movements.

## Section headings

Descriptive noun phrases naming the measure and the cut, as the `ui-ux-design`
skill already requires of the product. "Realised bond losses and the margin
gain", not "And it was spent". Never a verb-led headline, never a tease.

## What does not change

Bottom line first; every number sourced and traceable to `log.md`; published
figures quoted exactly and calculated ones carrying their formula; `pp` for a
change in a percentage; missing rendered as `—` and never as zero; charts titled
with their message and carrying a source line.
